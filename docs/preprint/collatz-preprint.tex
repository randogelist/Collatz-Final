\begin{abstract}
We document a sufficient finite-certificate route to the ordinary Collatz
conjecture. A terminating checker tests natural affine matrix interpretations
of a seven-symbol mixed binary--ternary rewriting system. The repository
proves in Lean that acceptance implies Collatz, serializes every accepted
certificate into a natural-number search space, and proves that symbolic
pruning preserves acceptance. The required project architecture is the forward
implication from accepted-witness existence; a converse is not required.
No accepted global certificate is supplied. We also describe Frontier A's
separate dyadic predecessor formulation: two explicitly identified iterated
limits commute exactly when Collatz holds. That interchange remains an open
premise, and its supporting source branch is outside the present repository
CI target.
\end{abstract}

\section{Main result and scope}
Throughout, $\NN=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. Let
\[
 C(n)=
 \begin{cases}
 n/2,&n\equiv0\pmod2,\\
 3n+1,&n\equiv1\pmod2,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \CC:\ \forall n\ge1,\ \exists t\in\NN,\ C^t(n)=1.
\]
For each actual dimension $m\ge1$, let $\mathcal W_m$ be the finite-table
certificate type defined in Section~2. The Boolean function $\chk$ is total
and consists entirely of finitely many exact natural-number comparisons.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-witness soundness]\label{thm:main}
The checked source establishes
\[
 \boxed{\bigl(\exists m\ge1,\ \exists W\in\mathcal W_m,\
                 \chk(W)=\mathrm{true}\bigr)\ \Longrightarrow\ \CC.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\source{\name{CHECKER\_SOUND}; \name{FINITE\_WITNESS\_IMPLIES\_COLLATZ}}

The implication contains no predecessor-density, analytic regularity,
trajectory-coverage, or termination premise. Its universal arithmetic transfer
is proved downstream of the finite check. Section~3 gives an exhaustive
natural-number encoding of this certificate language, and Section~4 removes
entire impossible families by symbolic proof.

The mathematical goal left by this architecture is concrete: exhibit one
finite table that passes the checker, and certify its admission in Lean.
Soundness does not itself establish that such a table exists. This preprint
reports a conditional proof architecture, not a proof of the Collatz conjecture.

\section{The finite matrix certificate}
The alphabet is
\[
 \Sigma=\{L,R,f,t,0,1,2\}.
\]
Its mixed binary--ternary encoding is taken from Yolcu, Aaronson, and
Heule~\cite{yah}. The following are the eleven oriented rules used by the
repository:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Role & Rules\\
\midrule
Dynamics & $fR\to R,\quad tR\to 2R$\\
Digit transport & $f0\to0f,\quad f1\to0t,\quad f2\to1f$\\
Digit transport & $t0\to1t,\quad t1\to2f,\quad t2\to2t$\\
Left boundary & $L0\to Lt,\quad L1\to Lff,\quad L2\to Lft$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A certificate $W\in\mathcal W_m$ assigns to every symbol $s$ a matrix
$M_s\in\NN^{m\times m}$ and a vector $b_s\in\NN^m$.
Write $F_s(x)=M_sx+b_s$. For a word $w=s_1\cdots s_\ell$, put
$F_w=F_{s_1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{s_\ell}$; thus the leftmost symbol acts last.
The empty word is the identity, and compilation uses
\[
 (M_A,b_A)\circ(M_B,b_B)=(M_AM_B,\ M_Ab_B+b_A).
\]
The checker accepts precisely when
\begin{align}
 (M_s)_{00}&\ge1 &&(s\in\Sigma),\label{eq:positive}\\
 M_{\mathrm{rhs}(r)}&\le M_{\mathrm{lhs}(r)},&
 b_{\mathrm{rhs}(r)}&\le b_{\mathrm{lhs}(r)}
 &&\hspace{-10mm}(r\text{ any rule}),\label{eq:weak}\\[-2pt]
 (b_{2R})_0&<(b_{tR})_0.\label{eq:strict}
\end{align}
In \eqref{eq:weak}, both comparisons are entrywise.
There are $7m^2+7m$ raw coefficients and
$7+11(m^2+m)+1$ scalar comparisons.
The propositional interface \name{Check W} is not a field of the raw witness.
\source{\name{check\_iff}; \name{toRanking}}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Define $\rho(w)=(F_w(0))_0\in\NN$. Nonnegative coefficients give monotonicity
in every coordinate; \eqref{eq:positive} preserves strict decrease in coordinate
zero through every prefix. Hence every rule weakly decreases $\rho$ in every
prefix and suffix context, while $tR\to2R$ strictly decreases it.

The source constructs binary numerals: $L$ represents $1$, and appending $f$
or $t$ represents $2n$ or $2n+1$. The nine auxiliary rules normalize
$3n+j$, $j\in\{0,1,2\}$, preserving the required weak rank inequality.
For the shortcut map $T(2n)=n$, $T(2n+1)=3n+2$, even steps are weakly
decreasing and nonbase odd steps are strictly decreasing. Strong induction
on rank, with structural induction on binary numerals for the even steps,
proves that every positive numeral reaches $1$. Binary coverage handles
every positive integer. Finally, each $T$ step is one or two ordinary $C$
steps, giving the stated conclusion.
\end{proof}
\source{\name{ranking\_implies\_collatz}; \name{shortcut\_implies\_full}}

\section{An exhaustive countable search}
The source convention \name{Witness d} means actual dimension $d+1$.
After excluding scalars, the executable raw format is simply
\[
 \Raw=\NN^3,\qquad R=(q,a,b).
\]
Here $q$ selects actual dimension $q+2$, while $a$ and $b$ encode all seven
matrices and all seven bias vectors. No proposition, proof object, oracle,
or infinite table is stored in $R$.

Use the pairing map
\[
 \pi(u,v)=\frac{(u+v)(u+v+1)}2+u,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{code}(q,a,b)=\pi(q,\pi(a,b)).
\]
The implementation defines the triangular number recursively and constructs a
diagonal decoder \name{unpair}. It proves
$\operatorname{unpair}(\pi(u,v))=(u,v)$. Recursive pairing therefore encodes
any finite table, with an exact decoding theorem.

The decoder fills each mandatory entry $(M_s)_{00}$ with its stored value
plus one. Packing an accepted certificate subtracts one from these seven
entries; positivity makes the reconstruction exact. Let
$\operatorname{candidate}:\NN\to\Raw$ be the nested decoder. Then
\[
 \operatorname{candidate}(\operatorname{code}(R))=R.
\]
This proves coverage of all raw candidates. Together with scalar exclusion
and the coupling guard of Section~4, it also proves coverage of every accepted
original certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Acceptance-preserving enumeration]
For the total executable checker $\ck$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \bigl(\exists n\in\NN,\ \ck(n)=\mathrm{true}\bigr)
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \bigl(\exists m\ge1,\ \exists W\in\mathcal W_m,\
                      \chk(W)=\mathrm{true}\bigr).}
\]
Consequently, $(\exists n,\ \ck(n)=\mathrm{true})\Longrightarrow\CC$.
\end{theorem}
\source{\name{INDEXED\_SEARCH\_IFF\_ORIGINAL\_ACCEPTANCE};
\name{INDEXED\_SEARCH\_IMPLIES\_COLLATZ}}

\begin{proof}
An accepted index decodes to an accepted matrix table. Conversely, every
accepted table is nonscalar, packs into the raw format, and is recovered by
decoding. The guard rejects no accepted table. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

The bounded routine checks the prefix below its bound, starting from the
largest index:
\[
 \finda(0)=\mathrm{none},\qquad
 \finda(b+1)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathrm{some}(b),&\ck(b)=\mathrm{true},\\
 \finda(b),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The source proves both
\begin{align*}
 \finda(b)=\mathrm{some}(n)&\ \Longrightarrow\
                 n<b\ \land\ \ck(n)=\mathrm{true},\\
 \ck(n)=\mathrm{true}&\ \Longrightarrow\
                 \finda(n+1)=\mathrm{some}(n).
\end{align*}
Thus a finite search succeeds exactly when this certificate language has an
accepted witness. Exhaustiveness is language-relative; it is not a guarantee
of success. The reference decoder is intentionally transparent and may be
impractical on large codes. No search-performance claim is made.

\section{Symbolic reductions of the witness space}
The exclusions below are proved for all coefficient values. They do not
result from testing a bounded list of candidates.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar obstruction]
No one-dimensional natural affine interpretation passes the checker.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $a,b,f,t,u$ be the slopes for $L,R,f,t,2$, respectively.
Write $\beta_s$ for the bias of symbol $s$.
The odd rule and the $L2$ rule imply
\begin{align*}
 ub&\le tb,& aft&\le au,\\
 a(f\beta_t+\beta_f)+\beta_L&\le a\beta_2+\beta_L,\\
 u\beta_R+\beta_2&<t\beta_R+\beta_t.
\end{align*}
Since $a,b,f\ge1$, cancellation and nonnegativity give
\[
 u\le t\le ft\le u.
\]
Therefore $u=t$. The weak bias inequality gives
$\beta_t\le f\beta_t+\beta_f\le\beta_2$, whereas the strict odd inequality
gives $\beta_2<\beta_t$. This is a contradiction.
\end{proof}
\source{\name{SCALAR\_CHECKER\_REJECTS};
\name{ACCEPTED\_DIMENSION\_POSITIVE}}

\begin{proposition}[Necessary coupling]
Every accepted certificate has
\[
 \exists s\in\{L,t,2\},\ \exists j>0:\ (M_s)_{0j}>0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose instead that the first rows of $M_L,M_t,M_2$ have no off-diagonal
entries. Their first coordinates then behave as scalar affine maps.
The remaining contributions from $M_f$ and $b_f$ are nonnegative and may
be discarded to obtain the scalar inequalities used above. The same
contradiction follows. No isolation condition is imposed on $M_f$, $M_R$,
or the other rows.
\end{proof}
\source{\name{THREE\_SCALAR\_ROWS\_IMPOSSIBLE};
\name{ACCEPTED\_REQUIRES\_FIRST\_ROW\_COUPLING}}

In particular, every fully diagonal interpretation is excluded.
Let $G(W)$ be the finite Boolean test of the displayed coupling condition.
The reduced checker satisfies the exact identity
\[
 G(W)\mathbin{\&\&}\chk(W)=\chk(W).
\]
The raw search therefore starts at dimension two, enforces the seven positive
leading entries by construction, and applies $G$ before the full checker.
These reductions preserve all accepted original certificates; no claim of
optimality or global minimality is made.

\subsection*{The remaining finite problem}
For some dimension $m\ge2$, find seven natural matrices and seven natural
vectors satisfying \eqref{eq:positive}--\eqref{eq:strict}. This one accepted
table would suffice for ordinary Collatz by the proved transfer.
A search engine may propose it, but admission must be checked for the exact
data and connected to the audited theorem. At the source snapshot documented
here, no such global witness has been supplied.

\section{Frontier A: conditional limit-exchange closure}
This section concerns the separate supporting archive~\cite{frontier}, not the
eight-module CI target. The construction uses the actual ordinary map $C$.

Let $B=\{n\ge1:\forall t,\ C^t(n)\ne1\}$. For $n>1$, define
\[
 \tau(n)=\min\{t:(C^t(n))^2\le n\}
\]
when that set is nonempty. Put $J(n)=C^{\tau(n)}(n)$ in that case, and
$J(n)=0$ otherwise; set $J(0)=0$, $J(1)=1$.
Zero marks genuine failure of a crossing, not a finite computational timeout.
For successful restarts of $n>1$, the arithmetic proof gives
\[
 0<J(n),\qquad J(n)^2\le n<4J(n)^2,\qquad J(n)<n.
\]
Define
\[
 A_k=\{n\ge1:J^k(n)=0\},
 \qquad U_c(j,k)=\one_{A_k}(2^jc)\quad(c\ge1).
\]
The sets $A_k$ increase, and $\bigcup_k A_k=B$: successful restarts of a bad
integer remain bad and strictly decrease, so they cannot continue forever;
good integers cannot encounter a failed restart.

\begin{theorem}[Both limit values]
For every positive $c$,
\[
 \lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}U_c(j,k)=\one_B(c),
 \qquad
 \lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}U_c(j,k)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The identity $C^j(2^jc)=c$ makes $2^jc$ bad exactly when $c$ is bad.
For each fixed $j$, monotone absorption therefore eventually equals
$\one_B(c)$ as $k$ increases.

For $P_c=\{n\ge1:\exists t,\ C^t(n)=c\}$, the supporting source proves
\[
 n\in P_c\cap A_k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n<(2c)^{2^k}.
\]
Thus $U_c(j,k)=1$ requires $2^jc<(2c)^{2^k}$. For each fixed $k$, this
fails at all sufficiently large $j$. One explicit sufficient cutoff in the
source is $j\ge(2c)^{2^k}$. The two outer limits are consequently constant.
\end{proof}
\source{\name{DEPTH\_THEN\_TOWER\_LIMIT};
\name{TOWER\_THEN\_DEPTH\_LIMIT} in \name{CollatzNativeLimits}}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional closure]
Equality of these two iterated limits for every $c\ge1$ is equivalent to $\CC$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equality forces $\one_B(c)=0$ for every positive $c$. Conversely, $\CC$ makes
the entire array zero.
\end{proof}

\textbf{The interchange premise remains unproved.}
Lean represents these limits by exact eventual equality of natural-valued
sequences; the ordinary real-limit interpretation follows for values $0,1$.
The restart and absorption predicates are noncomputable. This tower theorem
has no predecessor-density input. The original harmonic Frontier A instead
retains that separate input; these two formulations must not be conflated.
